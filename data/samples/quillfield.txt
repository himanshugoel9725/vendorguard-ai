Security & Trust Overview

Quillfield Analytics

Reporting period: 2025.

Quillfield Analytics turns files uploaded by its customers into interactive dashboards. Uploads may contain customer records.

Services: Dashboard builder for uploaded datasets; Scheduled report delivery.

Hosting: Northbound Cloud, US East region.

Data handled: employee and customer contact records; files and datasets uploaded by customers.

Independent reports held: SOC 2 Type II.Section 1: Governance

The sections below follow the order in which customers usually ask their questions.

A written security policy covers all staff, and management approval is renewed at each annual policy review.

The Chief Information Security Officer is accountable for information security and leads a dedicated security team.

Risk assessment is carried out ad hoc, and a risk register is being rolled out for risk management.

The company was founded to make routine work easier for its customers.Section 2: Access

Accounts for new staff are created on their first day at the company.

Every access request is approved by the data owner on a need to know basis, and RBAC groups define standard permissions.

Multi-factor authentication is mandatory for all staff, administrators and remote access, and single sign-on is required for production tools.

Admin access uses a break-glass procedure, and elevated privileges are granted only for the time needed.

An access review takes place once a year, and deprovisioning is handled manually for some systems.

Employees can reset forgotten passwords through the self-service page.Section 3: Data handling

Customers can export their own data from the portal at any time.

Disk encryption and KMS-managed keys protect all databases and file storage, in line with our encryption at rest standard.

All customer traffic uses encryption in transit with TLS 1.2 or higher, and HTTPS is enforced on every public endpoint.

A privacy programme exists and GDPR compliance work is in progress, with a data processing agreement drafted for some customers.

Backups are taken regularly and kept offsite, but restore tests are informal.

Data volumes grow every month as new customers join the platform.Section 4: Operations

The operations group looks after servers, databases and the tools that keep the service running.

Security-relevant events are sent to a central SIEM for logging, and audit logs are kept for twelve months under our log retention rule.

A patch management process applies updates monthly, and every CVE rated critical is tracked until closed.

A penetration test is not performed, and no independent security testing takes place.

A firewall protects the production network, and network segmentation is being rolled out between environments.

Servers are located in modern facilities with reliable power and cooling.Section 5: Resilience

Customers can follow the service status on a public page.

A documented incident response plan defines roles and severity levels, and a tabletop exercise tests it every year.

The company does not commit to notify customers of a data breach within a set time.

Staff are encouraged to keep their emergency contact details up to date.Section 6: Engineering

Engineers use modern programming languages and shared libraries.

Software follows a secure development lifecycle in which every change gets a code review and SAST runs in the pipeline.

Change approval is required for major releases, and rollback is tested ad hoc.

The engineering group meets every Monday to discuss the week.Section 7: Suppliers and assurance

Customers receive support in several languages.

The in-scope services hold a current certification, and an annual independent audit renews it.

Partner events are held twice a year to share product news.Section 8: People

New employees receive a laptop and meet their team during the first week.

Security awareness training is not performed, and no phishing simulation is run.

Social events and team lunches are organised every quarter.
