Security & Trust Overview

Brightdock Freight

Reporting period: 2025.

Brightdock Freight arranges freight forwarding and customs paperwork and offers a shipment tracking portal. It holds business contact details only.

Services: Freight forwarding; Customs documentation; Shipment tracking portal.

Hosting: Self-managed servers in a colocation facility.

Data handled: business contact details only.

Independent reports held: ISO/IEC 27001:2022.Section 1: Governance

This overview describes how the company organises its work and what customers can expect from it.

A written security policy covers all staff, and management approval is renewed at each annual policy review.

The Chief Information Security Officer is accountable for information security and leads a dedicated security team.

The company was founded to make routine work easier for its customers.Section 2: Access

Customers sign in through a web portal, and staff use company laptops for daily work.

Access to systems and customer data follows an access control policy based on least privilege and role-based access.

Multi-factor authentication is not enforced for staff accounts, and two-factor sign-in is optional.

Administrator accounts are restricted for most systems, and privileged access management tooling is being rolled out.

Employees can reset forgotten passwords through the self-service page.Section 3: Data handling

Customer data is stored in the region chosen by the customer at signup.

Databases use encryption at rest with AES-256, and disk encryption for file storage is on the roadmap.

All customer traffic uses encryption in transit with TLS 1.2 or higher, and HTTPS is enforced on every public endpoint.

A privacy programme exists and GDPR compliance work is in progress, with a data processing agreement drafted for some customers.

Point-in-time recovery is available for production databases, and backup encryption is applied to every copy.

Reports and dashboards are generated from the stored data on request.Section 4: Operations

New features are announced to customers in a monthly newsletter.

Logging is centralised for production systems, and security monitoring is in progress for the rest.

Vulnerability scanning is not performed, and there is no formal patch management process.

Production runs in a dedicated VPC with network segmentation, and a firewall controls every inbound connection.

Servers are located in modern facilities with reliable power and cooling.Section 5: Resilience

The service aims to be available whenever customers need it.

The company does not commit to notify customers of a data breach within a set time.

A disaster recovery plan exists with an RTO target, and a business continuity test is planned.

Staff are encouraged to keep their emergency contact details up to date.Section 6: Engineering

New features are designed by small teams that work closely with customers.

A secure development lifecycle is not in place, and code review is not performed for most changes.

The engineering group meets every Monday to discuss the week.Section 7: Suppliers and assurance

Customers receive support in several languages.

The in-scope services hold a current certification, and an annual independent audit renews it.

Purchasing of office supplies follows the company expense rules.Section 8: People

The company employs people in several countries and offers flexible working.

Security awareness training is not performed, and no phishing simulation is run.

Employees can join interest groups such as running and chess clubs.
